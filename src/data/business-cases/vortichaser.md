## Your equipment should work together as well as you planned it

Most chase setups grow one practical decision at a time. Add a camera. Improve the audio. Find a better way to stream. Build an overlay. Eventually, you have capable equipment connected by a workflow that only you understand—and occasionally, even you have questions.

VortiChaser brings those production decisions into a connected environment. Saved rigs organize your setups. Camera and audio mappings connect physical devices to logical feeds. Scenes arrange those feeds into your presentation, while overlays add the information and identity you want viewers to see.

That preparation should carry forward. Changing a camera assignment should not require rebuilding every scene that uses it. Going out again should not mean reconstructing a setup that worked last weekend. The platform is designed to make your operation repeatable, with room to refine it as your equipment and experience grow.

## Your broadcast doesn’t need to go home before it goes live

Sending field video to a home computer can work. It also adds another location, another internet connection, and another machine to troubleshoot while you are somewhere else.

VortiChaser’s native broadcast workflow removes the need to route your stream home to OBS just to produce the finished presentation. Studio brings feeds, scenes, overlays, preview, and broadcast controls together, while the desktop Broadcast Engine handles local media contribution.

Choose the rig, map the sources, arrange the production, set the output, and broadcast. The aim is control from the operation you brought with you, without depending on an unattended workstation in a spare bedroom.

When something needs attention, you should have useful information in front of you—not a remote desktop connection and a growing suspicion that the home computer restarted.

## Build your overlays around your chase

Your broadcast has its own identity. You know what information matters to your audience, where it belongs, and what should remain visible without covering the view.

VortiChaser’s widget builder lets you create elements for your overlays and use them across the connected network setup. Those widgets become part of the same production environment as your feeds and scenes, rather than another arrangement trapped on one computer.

Build the elements, organize the overlays, and use them in your presentation. As you improve the design, the platform gives that work a connected home. You should be able to spend your preparation time improving the broadcast instead of repeatedly copying the same configuration between tools.

## Show us what is happening under the hood

Chasers who build their rigs want meaningful controls. Which camera is assigned? Where is the audio coming from? What does the program look like? Is the stream actually reaching its destination?

VortiChaser brings preview, bitrate controls, and broadcast health information into the workflow so those questions have somewhere to be answered. Changing coverage and limited bandwidth are part of mobile broadcasting. Clear status and reconnection behavior help you understand what the system is doing with the connection available.

Continuity and recording remain areas of continuing development. Keeping a live picture moving and preserving footage for later delivery are different problems, and they deserve clear explanations of what works, what is being improved, and what resources each capability requires.

A useful system gives you enough visibility to make decisions. A reassuring green indicator that conceals a failed broadcast is merely decoration.

## We want to build better chase software. We don’t need to get rich off your subscription.

VortiChaser is a paid platform without an open-source edition. Video delivery, storage, infrastructure, maintenance, and development have costs. A service people depend on also needs enough financial stability to keep operating and improving.

**Our goal is to keep annual profit from subscriptions at no more than 10% of subscription revenue, after the costs of operating, supporting, administering, and developing the platform.** That remaining margin is intended to provide a modest cushion and help fund further research, development, and administrative needs as they arise.

That is a financial target we intend to explain and report against—not a vague claim that whatever remains must have been “reasonable.”

We know subscriptions compete with fuel, mobile data, equipment, and everything else you pay to get out there. Asking for part of that budget creates a responsibility to deliver value and be clear about where the money goes.

## If the price changes, the explanation comes with it

Our reasons for raising subscription prices should be straightforward: the services behind the platform cost more, or we need to fund clearly identified development that adds to what the platform can do.

**Whenever we raise subscription prices, we will document what led to that decision.** That explanation will identify the relevant operating-cost changes, development investment, or combination of the two, and explain what it means for subscribers.

If infrastructure costs increase, we will say so. If we are investing in a substantial new capability, we will explain what we are building and why it requires additional funding. Planned work will be described as planned work, rather than presented as something already delivered.

We also intend to provide a periodic summary of subscription spending and progress against our profit target. You should be able to see whether the service is following the principles used to sell it.

You do not need every invoice in our accounting system. You deserve an understandable answer to a reasonable question: **“Why are you asking me to pay more?”**
